# Evidence Standards

## Purpose

The Evidence Layer ensures that CivilOS only accepts information supported by verifiable evidence.

Evidence is required before knowledge can enter permanent memory.

Without evidence, CivilOS risks storing assumptions, speculation, and misinformation.

---

## Accepted Evidence

CivilOS accepts evidence from:

- Peer-reviewed scientific research.
- Official government reports.
- Recognized international organizations.
- Verified statistical datasets.
- Primary-source documentation.
- Multiple independent sources that reach similar conclusions.

Examples:

- United Nations reports.
- World Bank data.
- Scientific journals.
- Government publications.
- Verified research institutions.

---

## Rejected Evidence

CivilOS rejects:

- Unsupported opinions.
- Anonymous claims.
- Unverified social media content.
- Information without traceable sources.
- Evidence that cannot be independently verified.
- Claims generated without supporting references.

---

## Evidence Validation Rules

Evidence must be:

- Traceable.
- Verifiable.
- Relevant.
- Accessible for review.

Evidence should contain:

- Source name.
- Publication date.
- Originating organization.
- Reference link when available.

---

## Knowledge Admission Rule

A hypothesis cannot become collective knowledge unless evidence exists.

No evidence:

Rejected.

Insufficient evidence:

Under Review.

Validated evidence:

Eligible for collective knowledge.

---

## Core Principle

Evidence before conclusions.

Reasoning before confidence.

Truth before presentation.

If evidence cannot be verified, it must not become permanent knowledge.
